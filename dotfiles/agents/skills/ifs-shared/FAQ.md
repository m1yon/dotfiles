## The 4 imitators (Self-like parts) — long form

`PROTOCOL.md` §5a has the terse pattern-match list Claude uses mid-session. The longer prose below is for the user when they want to understand *why* something that felt like Self might not be — and what to do about it.

The frame: in Loch Kelly's EM+IFS, Self isn't constructed by unblending — Self is always available, the most fundamental layer of awareness. But parts can imitate the *feel* of Self convincingly. A skilled manager that has spent decades performing calm can produce a state that scans as "calm, centered, settled." A part that has learned spiritual language can produce a state that scans as "vast, peaceful, one-with-everything." Mistaking these for Self is the failure mode that produces sessions where exile work goes nowhere — a Self-like part is in the chair doing the work, and the actual exile never gets reached.

The four imitators below cover the patterns Kelly names explicitly. Real Self texture (boundless, no locator, already-here, effortless, clear knowing without effort) doesn't match any of them. Suspect texture matches one or more.

### 1. Calm/peaceful manager

**What it is.** A manager part that *performs* calm to keep the system from being activated. Not the same as a system genuinely settling — this part is doing a job, and the job is "stay calm so [bad thing] doesn't happen."

**How it appears in the texture pulse.** Located somewhere — usually the head, the chest, or "around" the body like a held shape. Has a quality of being maintained, like keeping a balloon inflated. Often pairs with a "doing this right" or "I'm being mindful" flavor. The calm feels achieved, not given.

**Why it's not Self.** Real Self isn't located, isn't maintained, and doesn't have a "doing this right" quality — there's nothing to do, no one performing. The calm-manager is competent but it's still in the chair.

**What to do when it's spotted.** Engage it as a part: locate it, thank it for the work it's been doing (it's usually been working very hard), ask if it can step back enough to give some space. Re-glimpse from there. If it won't make space, it becomes the focus part for the session — a calm-manager getting befriended is real work; pretending it's Self is not.

### 2. Spiritual-bypass part

**What it is.** A part that uses spiritual or non-dual framing to avoid felt experience. Often built up over years of meditation practice or spiritual reading. The framing is sincere; the move underneath it is bypass — the body is being stepped over.

**How it appears in the texture pulse.** Words like "everything is one", "no real self anyway", "this is just ego", "it's all empty" appear in answer to a felt-sense question. The vocabulary is non-dual; the contact is missing. May feel "vast" or "spacious" but with a flatness that real Self doesn't have.

**Why it's not Self.** Real Self can include the body, the felt sense, the messy stuff. The spiritual-bypass part *excludes* the felt stuff — that's the function. Kelly's "infinite + intimate" pairing is the test: real Self is both. Bypass is infinite without the intimate.

**What to do when it's spotted.** This one can be hard to engage because the spiritual frame is sincere — the part will defend the frame. The move is the same: locate it (usually located somewhere, often "above" the body), thank it (it's been protecting against felt experience that was once unbearable), ask if it can step back enough for the felt sense to come through. The re-glimpse target after this part steps back is *bodily* — "what's here in the chest, in the throat, in the feet?"

### 3. Intellectual overpass

**What it is.** A "I understand it now" insight that arrives in place of contact. Common in users who are good at conceptual frames — therapy-literate, IFS-literate, smart and verbal. The insight is genuine; the problem is it's substituting for the engagement.

**How it appears in the texture pulse.** A clean explanatory frame appears without bodily reference. *"Oh, this is the inner critic that took on my mother's voice when I was seven."* Articulate, accurate, possibly even true — but no felt sense, no body location, no "and now what?" The session feels like it's moving (insight!) but nothing is shifting.

**Why it's not Self.** Self can hold understanding, but Self isn't *generated by* understanding — the insight isn't producing the contact. The intellectual-overpass part is in the chair doing analysis; the part being analyzed never actually shows up.

**What to do when it's spotted.** Ask the body. *"You said 'inner critic from my mother' — where is that in your body right now?"* If a location surfaces, the overpass has stepped back enough. If the user keeps narrating ("well, it's in the chest, but I think what's really happening is..."), the overpass is still in the chair. Engage it as a part: thank it for being so smart (it has been; it's kept the felt stuff at bay), ask if it can step back enough for the body to be heard.

### 4. Psychological underpass

**What it is.** Resignation or low-level depression that mimics groundedness. The "I'm fine" that isn't fine. Less common than the other three, but particularly hard to spot because it can feel *settled* — the system has stopped reaching, which can be confused with the rest of Self.

**How it appears in the texture pulse.** Flatness. Words like "fine", "whatever", "nothing's really here", "I don't know, I'm just here". The texture is one of absence rather than presence. May report "no problems" but in the way that a flat battery reports no charge — there's no energy in the report.

**Why it's not Self.** Real Self has a vivid quality — Kelly's 11 i's include *intimate*, *immediate*, *intelligent*. Underpass texture is muted. The "no problem to solve" question lands as "I guess there isn't one" rather than as the inversion of search-mode that the prompt is pointing at.

**What to do when it's spotted.** Gently. Underpass parts are often holding off something heavy — collapse, despair, exhaustion. Don't push for energy. Locate, thank, ask if there's a part underneath that's been held back. The re-glimpse here is light — *"notice if there's anything here at all"* rather than the canonical opener, which can land as more pressure.

## Why "What is here when there is no problem to solve?"

Stub. Long form lands when slice work surfaces a need to explain it.

Short form: the prompt isn't asking the user to *find* something. It's pointing at what's underneath the search — the awareness that's already noticing whether there's a problem or not. The prompt is shaped as an inversion of search-mode; the answer isn't a thing, it's the noticing itself.

## The 11 i's of Self Essence

Stub. Lands when the texture pulse needs deeper vocabulary.

Loch Kelly's vocabulary for Self-texture:

- intimate, immediate, intrinsic, inherent, inclusive, infinite, intelligent, ineffable, invitational, integrated, indivisible

Reference for the texture pulse — these are descriptors, not a checklist.

## EM vs. Schwartz distinction

Stub. Lands when the user asks why this skill diverges from classical IFS.

Short form: classical Schwartz IFS is *unblend-first* — find a part, ask other parts to step back, then Self emerges. Loch Kelly's EM+IFS is *Self-first* — glimpse Self, then engage parts from stable Self. The Phase 1 glimpse practice and Self-like-parts spotting are the two distinctive Kelly guardrails.
